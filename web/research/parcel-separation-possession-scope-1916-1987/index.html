Parcel separation and possession scope, 1916–1987

This page sets out the corrected working model for the Great House Farm / Tŷ Mawr land history. It is intended to prevent the tenancy, parcel boundaries, possession orders, physical execution and later registrations from being collapsed into one undifferentiated “whole farm” narrative.

Reading rule: This is the family's corrected research reconstruction, not a judicial finding or a final determination of title. Each proposition is labelled by its evidential status. Original instruments, plans, court files and registration records remain the controlling material.

2. 1916 and 1928: the changing meaning of “whole farm”

The family reconstruction distinguishes the 1916 description of the farm from the position after 1928. The reported relevant term in the 2 May 1877 agreement took effect in 1928 and changed the parcel interests. The 1916 geographical meaning must not be carried forward into the 1955 order without examining the 1877 instrument, plan, 1928 evidence and contemporary rent records.

Primary research reference: EV-0014. The original 1877 agreement and plan remain to be linked to their verified catalogue record.

3. Frederick's tenancy and Mary's independent occupation

The corrected family position is that Frederick Buckler's agricultural tenancy related to Parcel B only. There was no tenancy of Parcel A. Mary Williams's occupation and asserted ownership of A2 (the farmhouse and garden) were independent of Frederick's tenancy.

The 1949 agricultural tenancy must therefore not be mapped to A1 or described as a tenancy of Great House Farm as a whole. Separate 1949 attempts to make Mary's occupation permissive are a distinct strand; the family account is that those attempts did not succeed. The offers, replies and solicitor records must be assessed independently.

4. The 1939 agreement: Parcel B only

The WGR–John Williams agreement and its plan are associated with Parcel B only; the plan does not depict the farmhouse and garden. The family reports that the signature attributed to John Williams does not match his signature and that the consideration was not paid. These are authenticity, execution and performance questions. The original document, provenance, signature exemplars and payment records are required; no conclusion of forgery or deliberate misrepresentation is made on this page.

Research reference: EV-0023.

Research references: EV-0032, EV-0034 and EV-0080.

6. The 1962 possession proceedings

The family's case is that the 1962 possession order relied on ambiguity in the 1955 order and concerned a different parcel from the land covered by the 1939 Parcel B agreement. This must be tested against the original 1962 claim, particulars, defence, order, plan, transcript and any mesne-profits schedule. The 1987 judgment's recital is not a substitute for those instruments.

Research reference: EV-0041.

7. The 1982–87 registrations and litigation

WA231076 (1982) and WA240304 (1983) must be analysed separately, with their own applications, root instruments, filed plans, statements of possession, notices and service records. The family disputes whether the title cited in the 1987 judgment established the farmhouse parcel later registered in 1983, and whether Mary received notice or a meaningful opportunity to object. The 2 February 1987 amalgamation must be placed accurately in the litigation chronology; it must not be used to rewrite the earlier separate title history.

The 1987 Court of Appeal outcome remains the operative judicial result unless set aside. This research asks whether the underlying parcel and title premises were correctly identified; it does not silently replace the court's decision.

8. Frequently asked questions

Was Frederick Buckler a tenant of the whole farm?

No. The corrected research position is that his agricultural tenancy related to Parcel B only. It does not establish a tenancy of Parcel A.

Was Mary Williams a tenant of Parcel A?

No tenancy of Parcel A is identified in the corrected model. Mary's occupation and asserted ownership position in the farmhouse and garden (A2) are treated independently of Frederick's tenancy.

What did the 1939 WGR–John Williams agreement cover?

The plan is associated with Parcel B only and does not depict the farmhouse and garden. The attributed signature, execution and payment of consideration remain disputed documentary questions.

Did the 1955 possession order cover everything physically taken?

The family reconstruction distinguishes Order 14's legal scope (Parcel B only) from the physical taking on 4 July 1955 (Parcel B, Parcel C and northern woodland A1). The farmhouse and garden (A2) remained occupied by Mary. The original order and execution return must be compared to establish the position independently.

What is the concern about the 1962 possession order?

The family case is that the 1962 order relied on ambiguity in the 1955 order and related to a different parcel from the 1939 agreement. The original pleadings, order and plan are needed before drawing a legal conclusion.

Did the House of Lords hear a full appeal?

No substantive House of Lords appeal hearing has been identified. Leave to appeal was refused on 3 March 1988. The petition and committee papers are required to establish the grounds presented and the basis of the refusal.
