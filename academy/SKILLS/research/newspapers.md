# Skill: newspapers and press archives

**Use when:** looking for reports of a sale, a court action, a demolition or a statement.

1. Start from a named event and date, then search the papers for that week (sales, hearings and announcements cluster).
2. Save each article's text with publication, date and page, so later re-reads do not depend on the site being reachable.
3. Cite the report as a report: what was printed, by whom, when.
4. Business press (company results, mortgage debentures, clearances) is often the only public trace of land changing hands between companies.
5. Record against-the-case items found on the way, in the same report.

## Welsh Newspapers (the national library's 1804–1919 papers), notes from 7 Nov 2026
- The first page load of a session often answers with a challenge page: lead each batch with a throw-away search, then run the real ones.
- Plain words are scored as OR. Join words and quoted phrases with AND to require all of them (Llandough AND overseers AND Williams), add a year range, and page through results; the article header gives the title, dated issue and page.
- Inclosure Commissioners' exchange notices on the front pages of the Cardiff weeklies (1845–1900) name freeholders by parish with tithe-map numbers: a route to who else held land in a parish.
- A "Mr Williams, Llandough" with no farm named is not Tŷ Mawr (Great House): a Williams household at Llandough Farm, a separate farm, were estate tenants in 1895. Attach a person to Tŷ Mawr only when the report names it.
- The site already carries a 1900–1919 decade sweep of these papers: check the existing registers before writing a row.
