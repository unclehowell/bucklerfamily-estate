# UK bookshop campaign hooks for a print copy: Independent Bookshop Week + Bookshop Day (guest bookselling)

Date: 4 Nov 2026 (harvest). Topic: getting a Cedwyn print edition into local indie bookshops through the two national trade campaigns, instead of a cold "please stock my book" ask. Builds on local-shop-consignment-paperback-sale-or-return-uk.md (terms) and bookshop-org-uk-affiliate-ebooks-d2d.md (online indie shelf); this file is the calendar + in-store activity layer.

## What the primary sources state (read 4 Nov 2026)
- Independent Bookshop Week (IBW), Booksellers Association: returns **Sat 12 - Sat 19 June 2027** (2026 was its 20th year). **800+ independent bookshops registered**. Publishers are invited to offer members "signed stock, indie exclusive editions, early releases, added value items, author signings and events".
  - Activities named on the BA page: **Guest Bookselling** ("for an hour, a morning or afternoon, or a full day ... a photo opportunity, and a chance for authors to sign some copies of their books in-store"); **Indie Twinning** (an independent publisher and an independent shop build a week-long programme to "drive local media coverage, footfall and social media content"); **Bookshop Crawl**; **Big Book Club** (Wednesday of the week, in-person clubs); National Book Tokens "High Five" £5 e-gift flyers handed out by every participating shop.
- Bookshop Day (Books Are My Bag): **second Saturday in October** (page-stated), about **1,000 UK retailers** (indies + chain branches); 2026 was Sat 10 Oct (passed), so **next is Sat 9 Oct 2027**. Aim: start Christmas book-shopping early on the high street. Launched 2013. The BA page read lists no per-shop activity programme, so any signing is arranged shop by shop.
- Practitioner testimony, Roz Morris (ALLi, indie author): she asked shops "Do you stock **local** authors?" rather than "self-published", showed the book, "and I was in" at both of her local indies; one shop owner who read the book then hand-sold it at the till and hosted signings. Not numbers, but a tested opening line.
- Troubador (publisher guidance, 1 May 2026): shops buy on **sale or return**, want a proven distribution route, a **printed cover price in the barcode**, a pub date, professional cover/typesetting; they are **unwilling to stock print-on-demand-only titles** because POD returns are awkward. A copy in hand at the pitch helps.

## How to do it (sequence)
1. Gate: there must be a real print copy (short print run or a POD title that is returnable via the distributor), price on the back cover, ISBN barcode. No print copy = park this skill.
2. List target shops: indie bookshops within ~25 miles that stock local/Welsh history (check each shop's own site/Instagram for a "local authors" shelf). Also check bookshops.org / BA "find a bookshop".
3. Approach in person or one short email (as a HubSpot draft only): "local author, local history title, here is a copy, terms are sale or return at X% discount, delivered by hand". Lead with the local angle, not the self-published angle.
4. Offer a dated in-store moment tied to the campaign: a Guest Bookselling hour during IBW week or a signing on Bookshop Day. It gives the shop content for its own campaign posts, which is what they want from the week.
5. Ask the shop to tag the campaign (#IndependentBookshopWeek / #BookshopDay) and post our own photo the same day; link only to our /ebooks/ page for the e-book.
6. Log per shop: copies left, sold at 4 weeks, returned. Restock only shops that sell >=2 in the first 4 weeks (our own rule of thumb, not a source figure).

## Counter-read
- Neither campaign publishes sales uplift per author event; the reach numbers (800+ shops, ~1,000 retailers) are shops registered, not shoppers. Expect a handful of copies per shop, not a spike.
- Print economics (discount 35-40%+ plus print cost) can leave little per copy; the value is visibility and the shop's own social reach.
