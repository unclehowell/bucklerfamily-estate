# Shortlist of three: AI citations for course and learner pages (UPCEA + Search Influence 2026)

Read 4 Nov 2026. Topic: how adult learners choose online/continuing-education programmes now that AI overviews shape a very short shortlist, and what a course or academy page must do to get on it.

## The evidence (with numbers)
UPCEA and Search Influence, "AI Search in Higher Education: How Prospects Search in 2026" (press release 16 Sep 2026; survey of 1,057 adults aged 18-60 interested in continuing/online education, fielded 5-14 May 2026; US association, US-led sample):
- Over half of prospects considered three programmes or fewer before applying.
- Search engines are still the main starting point (80%); 54% also use AI tools during the decision.
- 83% read AI-generated overviews when they appear.
- 64% say being cited as a source in an AI answer makes them trust that institution more (56% in 2025).
- Share who always click through to a cited source nearly doubled: 11% -> 19%.
- 86% report at least one concern about AI search accuracy (68% in 2025): learners use AI more and check it harder.
- UPCEA's Bruce Etter: "a citation in an AI answer can truly be the deciding factor for an institution to make a learner's increasingly small shortlist." Search Influence's Paula French: prioritise "accurate, authoritative content that AI can confidently cite", visible in both organic and AI results.
- Caveat: a survey of stated behaviour from an agency that sells AI-search services; the full report download page returned 404 on 4 Nov, so only the press-release figures are used here.

## How to do it
1. Write the course page so an AI can quote it accurately: first 2-3 sentences answer who it is for, what you learn, format, length, price, and what you get at the end. Put these as visible text (not only in images or JS), plus Course schema (name, description, provider, offers with price and currency, hasCourseInstance with mode online).
2. Because 86% worry about accuracy, show proof beside every claim: sample lesson, sources list, who made it, last-updated date.
3. Be the source AI cites for the question, not just the course: a free, well-sourced explainer on the topic (the question a learner asks first) with a clear link to the course.
4. Test monthly: ask 3-5 AI tools the 5 questions a learner would ask ("best online course on X", "how to learn X history"). Record cited yes/no and whether facts about you are right; fix wrong facts on your own page first.
5. Track click-through from AI referrers to the course page separately (19% of prospects always click a cited source; those visits are warm).
6. Assume a three-option shortlist: compare yourself honestly against the two most likely alternatives and say on the page what is different (free tier, primary records, local knowledge).
