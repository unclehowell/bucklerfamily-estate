# Wassail and the orchard calendar: Twelfth Night / Old Twelfth Night (Jan) and Apple Day (21 Oct) as heritage hooks

Harvested 2026-11-05. Before this file: 'wassail', 'orchard', 'Twelfth Night', 'Apple Day' had 0 hits in INDEX.md (Calan Gaeaf, Mari Lwyd, Hen Galan and Plygain are covered elsewhere; this file is the orchard/farm side of the same winter calendar).

## What the sources show
- **Glamorgan had its own wassail tradition, with a local object to show for it.** Museum Wales (Collections Online, read 5 Nov 2026), wassail bowl item 24.276, Ewenny Pottery: "The ancient New Year custom of wassailing was practiced in Glamorgan, as in other parts of Wales in the nineteenth century, when parties of wassailers would progress from house to house with traditional greetings for the health and prosperity of the inhabitants". Bowl item 67.349.1 (Claypits Pottery, Ewenny, c.1910, from the Oriel 1 gallery text at St Fagans): carried "from door to door on New Year's Day or Twelfth Night"; "The elaborately decorated earthenware bowls made at Ewenny were locally famous." Ewenny (near Bridgend) is in the same old county as Llandough, some 15-20 miles west (distance not measured).
- **Dates.** Twelfth Night = 5 Jan (some count 6 Jan); "Old Twelfth Night" = 17 Jan (pre-1752 calendar). Live 2027 listings found by search (snippets, not opened): Shoemakers Museum orchard wassail Sun 17 Jan 2027, 2-4 pm, free with a pass; North End Farm Sat 16 Jan 2027; Dunham Apples Sat 9 Jan 2027. So the posting window is roughly 2-17 Jan, after the Christmas noise and before Dwynwen (25 Jan, covered elsewhere).
- **Demand signal (testimony, no audited numbers).** Organisers repeatedly report sell-outs: a community site's post "selling out of tickets in record time ... the most successful wassailing to date" (Facebook snippet, Hutton community, not opened); a Somerset Rural Life Museum ticketed evening wassail (snippet). No attendance or conversion number was found; treat any reach claim as not checked.
- **Apple Day (autumn twin).** Common Ground (read): launched 21 Oct 1990 in Covent Garden, "Forty stalls were taken"; now used by WI groups, National Trust properties, museums, horticultural societies and schools. Wikipedia (citing Common Ground): "By 2000 the day was celebrated in more than 600 events around the United Kingdom." Events usually fall on an October weekend.
- **Museum practice.** St Fagans uses the wassail bowl in gallery text and runs Mari Lwyd performances in the same season (museum.wales what's-on, snippet); museums post the object plus the custom on social each January (Museum Wales Instagram post on the 1910 Ewenny bowl, snippet).

## How to do it (zero cost)
1. **Object-led post, not a sales post.** One image of a public-domain or openly licensed source (or our own photo of the farm in winter), a two-line custom explanation, and one sentence tying it to the place: what a Glamorgan farm's year looked like at the turning of the year. Credit museum images exactly as the museum asks ("By permission of Amgueddfa Cymru — Museum Wales"); never re-host an image whose licence we have not read.
2. **Two posting moments:** 5-6 Jan (Twelfth Night) and 15-17 Jan (Old Twelfth Night, the date most English orchard wassails use). One post each, plus a reply-led follow-up only where people engage.
3. **Join existing conversations:** #Wassail / #Wassailing on Instagram, Facebook and Bluesky in that window; #AppleDay around 21 Oct. Reply to museum and orchard posts with a single relevant record line, not a link.
4. **Apple Day (Oct 2027):** if the farm records name orchards, varieties or cider, an orchard-themed record post on 21 Oct; list any related talk on local what's-on sites (see local-whats-on-listings-newsquest-evvnt-cardiff.md).
5. **Measure:** UTM `utm_campaign=wassail2027` (or `appleday2027`) on the single link in bio/first comment; compare with the same weekday the week before. Kill rule: under 10 clicks across both January posts means drop it for 2028.
