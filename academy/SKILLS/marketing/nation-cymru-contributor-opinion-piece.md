# Nation.Cymru contributor opinion piece (500-800 words, timely hook)

Written 2026-11-04 by the skills harvest. Grep before writing: "nation.cymru" appeared only as a passing mention in 2 skills (talks circuit, St David's Day). No skill covered its contributor route.

## What it is (primary, read 4 Nov 2026)
- Nation.Cymru's contact page: "Our platform attracts an audience of over one million readers a month and publishes opinion pieces from across the political spectrum. Join the national conversation – write for us today!" The reach figure is the site's own, not audited; not checked.
- It is volunteer-run, and the contributor guide (PDF by editor Ifan Morgan Jones) wants pieces on "anything that touches in any way upon the challenges and opportunities facing Wales as a nation ... history, culture ... tourism".
- The guide's rules:
  - find a timely hook in current news;
  - 500 to 800 words ("research suggests that the maximum most people will read on a screen is 800 words");
  - state the argument at the start;
  - accessible language;
  - punch up, not down.
- A separate guide covers news stories: keep yourself and your opinion out, and quote someone else.
- Contact page process:
  - say clearly if the piece is time-sensitive or must run by a date;
  - expect a slow reply ("large volume of emails");
  - send news tips and press releases to the news inbox;
  - features go to the features editor (David Owens);
  - news goes to the news editor (Emily Price).
  - The addresses are Cloudflare-obfuscated on the page, so read them live when drafting.
- It is an IPSO member and follows the Editors' Code. Anything we send must be accurate and fair to named people.
- It runs a feed (nation.cymru 10 items, read live), so published pieces also travel by RSS.

## How to do it
1. Pick a hook that is in the news this week (heritage at risk, planning, a Cadw/Heneb listing story, a Vale housing decision, the Welsh Patagonia anniversary). No hook, no pitch.
2. Write 550 to 750 words.
   - First paragraph: the argument in one sentence.
   - Then 3 points, each backed by a primary record (tithe map, census, newspaper).
   - Close with what it means for Wales.
   - Plain language, no jargon.
4. Email it as a HubSpot draft task. Subject: "Opinion: <headline> (time-sensitive: <date>)" if a date applies. Put the text inline, with 1 or 2 lines on why it is timely. the project lead sends it on GO.
5. If it runs: archive.org capture the page, log the referral clicks (UTM-free; read the cedwyn referrer in the Cloudflare analytics), and turn it into the "as featured" line only if the editor agrees.

## How we apply it to Cedwyn
- Best fit: a public-history piece on what the 1841 Llandough tithe apportionment shows about how the parish's land was held, hooked to the next heritage/planning news story in the Vale. It uses the Tithe H3 work already built, with no accusations and no family holdings.
- Second fit, near 28 Jul (Mimosa anniversary): a piece on the Welsh Patagonia link, hooked to the anniversary, with no money words in Argentina scope.
- Gate: press goes out on the project lead's GO only. The draft lives in HubSpot. No email is sent from here. Under the editorial rules, banned words stay out of the piece too.
