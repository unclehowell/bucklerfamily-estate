# Reader discovery channels 2026: what 3,589 active e-book readers say (Written Word Media survey)

Harvested 2026-11-07 (SKILLS HARVEST). Primary source read in full: Written Word Media, "2026 Reader Survey: What Readers Want, Buy, and Expect", Tara Abbott, 2 Feb 2026, writtenwordmedia.com

## What the data states (self-reported; sample = WWM's own promo-newsletter readers, so email is over-weighted)
- Sample: 3,589 readers; >1 in 3 read 100+ books in 2025; median 6 books/month.
- Spend: median $15/month on books (mean $29.56); most pay under $5 per e-book; 26% in Kindle Unlimited; 25% prefer free downloads.
- Formats: 76% read e-books (61% e-reader, 33% tablet, 30% phone); 51% also read print; 22% audiobooks.
- Discovery (select all): Amazon 68%, email newsletters 64%, Goodreads 46%, friends/family 45%, social media 42%, author websites 30%, libraries 29%, bookstores 27%, AI tools <1%.
- AI: 71% never use ChatGPT/Gemini to find books, 21% curious, 7% occasional, <1% regular.
- Direct sales: 56% no preference where they buy, 35% prefer big retailers, 9% prefer buying direct; 28% rate "supporting indie authors" 10/10 — they buy direct when told it helps and checkout is smooth.
- Special editions: 30% have bought one; 23% would pay more for bonus content, commentary or early access.
- Reviews: 22% review often, 30% sometimes, 17% never; >50% track reading (Goodreads/Kindle).
- What readers want from authors: future projects 67%, story insights 59%, recommendations 59%, backstory 36%, process 19%.
- Where they follow authors: newsletter 49%, Facebook 38%, TikTok 2%.
- Audio: 80%+ prefer human narrators; <1% strongly prefer AI voices.
- Retention: 80%+ "extremely likely" to read more by an author they enjoyed (readthrough).

## Caveats (say these when quoting)
- Vendor survey of its own deal-newsletter audience, US-heavy, fiction-heavy; not checked against an independent panel. Use as direction, not as UK local-history fact.
- "AI tools <1%" is discovery by readers in this audience, not AI crawler visibility; keep AI-visibility work, but do not expect sales from it yet.

## Playbook (how to use it)
1. Rank channel effort by where readers actually look: retailer listing + email list first, Goodreads/catalogue presence second, Facebook third, TikTok last.
2. Every social bio/post points to ONE next step: the email signup or the product page (WWM's own tip).
3. Direct sales: say plainly why buying here helps ("buying here keeps the archive work going") and keep checkout to one tap; never make direct the only route if a retailer listing exists.
4. Offer an enhanced edition (bonus documents, commentary, early access) as the top tier; 23% say they'd pay more.
5. Back-matter review ask + Goodreads page kept current; half of readers track there.
6. Newsletter content = upcoming projects, behind-the-record insights, recommendations (top three asks), not process diaries.
7. Audio: if ever made, human-narrated; AI narration is a reader turn-off in this sample.

## How we apply it to Cedwyn
- Email first: the Mailchimp list had 0 campaigns sent (harvest note 5 Nov). The single highest-leverage step stays: one welcome + monthly "what's next in the archive" email (future projects = 67% want). Put the signup on /ebooks/ and every sample PDF end page.
- Goodreads: check each Cedwyn e-book has a Goodreads/StoryGraph record (46% discover there); a PROPOSAL if missing (listing needs the owner's account).
- Facebook (Great House Farm II) over TikTok: keep TikTok parked (2% follow authors there); FB posts end with the /ebooks/ or signup link, not a like ask.
- /ebooks/ page: one line on why buying direct helps the project; Stripe/Gumroad one-tap; fits the version standard (latest paid, older cheaper, v0.0.1 free) as the "value" ladder this price-sensitive audience wants.
- Enhanced edition = the latest paid version with extra scanned records/commentary; frame it as the special edition.
- Do not spend effort chasing "AI tool" book discovery for sales; AI-visibility flywheels stay for traffic/citations only.
- Any future audio/documentary narration: human voices (consistent with the movie's OmniHuman/Welsh-accent rules).
